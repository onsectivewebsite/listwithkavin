01
SW Calgary · Luxury Estates

Aspen Woods

Mountain-view modernism with a private-school commute and a champagne fridge in every kitchen.

Aspen Woods is where Calgary's executives and out-of-province transplants quietly settle. Stone facades, Wolf ranges, and three-car garages are table stakes. What makes it different is the calm — winding ridge streets, mature spruce, and direct access to Webber Academy, Calgary Academy, and Rundle College.

Resale value has outperformed the citywide average every year since 2019. Inventory is thin and rarely lasts. Off-market activity is significant here — many of Kavin's Aspen Woods buyers never see their winning home on MLS.

02
Downtown · Urban Cool

Beltline

Calgary's densest, most caffeinated neighborhood. Walk Score 98, and a different cocktail bar every Thursday.

Bounded by 17th Avenue's nightlife to the north and 11th Avenue's Mission-bound restaurants to the south, the Beltline is a condo dweller's playground. Towers like The Guardian, Park Point, and Royal stand on streets older Calgarians remember as walk-up apartments and industrial blocks.

For investors: the Beltline still delivers the city's most consistent rental yields. For owner-occupiers: pick your building carefully — concrete construction, mature condo boards, and full-time concierge are worth paying for.

NW Calgary · Family Suburb

Tuscany

Big lots, school catchments people fight over, and a Crowchild commute that still beats anything in Vancouver.

Tuscany is Calgary's quintessential family neighborhood. Built in the 1990s on a sweep of NW ridge land, it has held its value better than almost any comparable suburb thanks to the Tuscany Club, an active homeowners association, and walking access to the Crowchild C-Train station — rare for the deep northwest.

Best for first-time move-up buyers and growing families. Bungalows and two-storeys both available. Ridge homes with downtown / mountain sightlines command premiums.

04
SE Calgary · Lake Community

Mahogany

Year-round lake access in a city that gets to −30°C. Calgary's most ambitious master-planned community, and it works.

Built by Hopewell on a 63-acre freshwater lake, Mahogany has redefined Calgary's family-suburb category since 2010. The Mahogany Beach Club is the marquee amenity — a private 22-acre park, beach, and ice rink open only to residents. Eight years from launch to selling out is record pace for Calgary.

The lake premium widens, not narrows, every year. Lake-facing homes command a 12–18% surcharge over identical interior comps. Resale depth is strong; new construction is still active in the surrounding pockets.

05
Inner City NE · Reborn

Bridgeland

Calgary's quietly most-desirable inner-city neighborhood. Walk to the office, bike the river, eat like you're in Lisbon.

Once a working-class Italian and Ukrainian enclave, Bridgeland has been reborn through a decade of careful infill. The 1st Avenue spine — OEB Breakfast, Lukes Drug Mart, Phil & Sebastian, Una Pizza — is now one of Calgary's most-walked restaurant rows. The Bow River pathway is at the doorstep.

Mix of original 1920s cottages, mid-century apartments, and architect-designed 2010s infills. Rental yields are competitive with the Beltline; capital appreciation has been stronger.

06
Historic Inner City · Heritage

Inglewood

Calgary's original main street, still pulling artists, restaurateurs, and architects three generations later.

Inglewood was Calgary's first neighborhood, settled in 1875. Today, 9th Avenue SE is the city's most cinematic stroll — vintage stores, brewpubs, vinyl shops, indie music venues, and ten-year-waitlist restaurants like Rouge.

Housing stock ranges from restored 1910s craftsmans to architect-designed modern infills. Heritage protection on parts of the avenue keeps the streetscape preserved. Bird sanctuary at the southern edge.

07
SW Calgary · Heritage Estate

Elbow Park

Calgary's old money, still gracefully old money. Tudors, river paths, and a tennis club your grandfather joined.

Elbow Park is Calgary's most prestigious mature neighborhood — large lots, mature trees, and homes that have stayed in families for generations. The Elbow River pathway and Glencoe Club anchor the lifestyle. Restored 1910s estates trade for $2M–$5M; redevelopment lots fetch a million plus before construction starts.

Inventory is famously thin. Most homes sell off-market or through realtor networks. Kavin has placed buyers in 14 Elbow Park homes — only six of which were ever publicly listed.

08
SW Calgary · Top-Rated Schools

West Springs

School catchments people relocate from other provinces for, and a Saturday morning Aspen Landing rhythm.

Built in the early 2000s on the western edge of Calgary, West Springs delivers what executive families travel across the country for: West Springs School, Olympic Heights, and a short commute to Webber Academy and Calgary Academy. Lots are bigger than newer southwest developments. Streets curve, the topography rolls.

Resale velocity is strong year-round; spring listings often sell in under three weeks. Bonus rooms, triple garages, and finished basements are standard.
